NYC After Dark

How much electricity does New York use, and waste, between 1 and 5 a.m.?

Sources & methods

The data

SourceWhat it isCovers
NYISO measured Hourly electricity load for Zone J (New York City) and day-ahead prices. Everything on the grid: homes, offices, subways, hospitals. Jan 2016 to yesterday; later days fetched live
NREL ComStock modeled Simulated hourly lighting and electricity for today's commercial buildings, by borough and building type, as built and with a Lighting Controls retrofit (occupancy sensors and similar). One typical year (2018 weather); 2025 release 3. Can't show change over time.

Definitions and assumptions

  • Overnight is 1–5 a.m. local time: the four hours starting at 1, 2, 3 and 4 a.m.
  • "Waste" has three levels. (1) All overnight grid load: measured, but it includes homes, hospitals and subways, so it's a ceiling, not waste. (2) Commercial lighting on at night: modeled. (3) Avoidable lighting: level 2 minus what a lighting-controls retrofit or a lights-out rule would leave on.
  • Lights-out rule: 20% of overnight lighting is assumed to stay on for exits, security and night shifts. That's an assumption, not a measurement, and answers call it one.
  • The lighting map spreads ComStock's lighting per square foot (by borough and building type) over each hexagon's commercial floor area from PLUTO. It places about 94% of the citywide modeled total; use it for where, and the citywide figures for how much.
  • Hexagons are H3 resolution 9, about 350 m across. Satellite values are interpolated from the four nearest pixels, so neighboring hexagons share data.

How an answer is made

  • A Lead agent asks you to clarify if the question is vague, then splits it into 2–4 tasks.
  • Researchers, each starting fresh with only its task and one slice of the data, run read-only SQL in parallel. Each query gets an id like Q3. The Lead can send more researchers to fill gaps (8 at most).
  • A Writer turns the findings into the answer and picks a map layer. The Sources list under each answer is built from the log of queries actually run, not by the AI, and flags any id that doesn't exist.
  • Research stops after 3 minutes; the answer then uses whatever findings are in and says so.
  • "How I got this" under each answer shows every query and its result; "Agent timeline" shows exactly what each agent saw.

What it can't tell you

  • Which building leaves its lights on: there is no building-level night-time data.
  • Home lighting, streetlights, restaurants, factories or data centers on their own: they aren't modeled.
  • How modeled lighting changed over time: ComStock describes one typical year.
